Another Week, another round. 

---

Mansour Alhmoud wrote up what GroupIB was able to learn about a threat actor they call DarkLunnen through an open server directory. At times they were apparently able to follow the Russian-speaking operator live. The exposure was eventually noticed and the open directory was closed, but before that GroupIB managed to download 3.1 GB of data.
- https://www.group-ib.com/blog/offensive-cyber-operations-europe/

---
Outflank has turned ten and shares the interesting story of how the small team got here, with no departures in those ten years.
- https://www.outflank.nl/blog/2026/11/11/a-decade-of-evasion-celebrating-10-years-of-outflank/

---
Unit42 found that the Falcon (aka XG or Xiàng Gē) C2 framework was used to compromise several government bodies in the Middle East and Southeast Asia through malicious JavaScript. There also seem to be considerable overlaps in the approach with the PeckBirdy framework.
- https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/falcon-c2-browser-compromise/

---
Umut Bayram from Picus wrote up the tactics of the group Imperial Kitten. The group mainly targets the aviation and defense sectors and is closely tied to Iran. They rely on several phishing and spearphishing phases, both for initial access and for moving laterally through the network with collected IT credentials.
- https://www.picussecurity.com/resource/blog/imperial-kitten-tortoiseshell-apt-ttps-malware-and-attack-simulation

---
The Citrus malware is spreading through companies using stolen credentials from service providers. Stanislav Larinsky, Vladislav Vlasov, and Boris Trefilov from Kaspersky's Securelist examined the malware's behavior. It uses both classic tools like Mimikatz and keyloggers, as well as built-in Windows tools such as wevtutil.
- https://securelist.com/citrus-ransomware/121879/

---
Kush Pandya analyzed a new malicious Ruby gem for Socket. It not only compromises the affected system but also spreads further. JavaScript is loaded during gem installation and, among other things, reads 51 credential stores.
- https://socket.dev/blog/shai-hulud-descendant-malicious-ruby-gem

---
There apparently are routers that look like Minions. Klogix took them apart, looked at the firmware, and found a few vulnerabilities.
- https://www.klogixsecurity.com/scorpion-labs-blog/anatomy-of-an-iot-exploit-stealing-the-moon
